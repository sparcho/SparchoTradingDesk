Plain-English Glossary

Geospatial briefs accumulate jargon — regulators, sensor types, programs. This page expands every

abbreviation and technical term used in the document so the reader can follow the argument without

specialist context. Terms are grouped by category. The page is dense by design; everything else is written

in plain English on top of these definitions.

Geospatial Domain Terms

GIS

Geographic Information System. Software + data + analytics for anything spatial — maps,

satellite imagery, location intelligence, terrain, infrastructure layouts.

EO

Earth Observation. Imagery and measurements collected from satellites or aerial platforms.

Includes optical, hyperspectral, SAR, multispectral data.

Hyperspectral

Imaging across hundreds of narrow wavelength bands (vs. RGB / 3-band optical). Lets you

identify materials — minerals, crops, pollutants — by their spectral signature. Pixxel's

speciality.

SAR

Synthetic Aperture Radar. Active-sensor satellite imagery using microwaves. Works through

clouds and at night. Critical for monitoring (defence, maritime, disaster). PierSight's speciality.

Multispectral

Imaging across a handful of broad wavelength bands (e.g. visible + near-infrared +

short-wave infrared). Standard for vegetation / land-use monitoring.

Optical (satellite)

Visible-light satellite imagery, like Google-Earth-grade pictures. Standard reference layer.

UAV

Unmanned Aerial Vehicle. Drone. Class includes tactical (military), industrial (surveying /

inspection), and consumer.

ADAS

Advanced Driver-Assistance Systems. Auto-industry term for sensor + software stacks

providing adaptive cruise, lane-keep, automated braking, etc. Demands HD-quality maps.

HD-ADAS / HD maps

High-definition mapping data tuned to ADAS requirements — centimetre-grade road

geometry, lane-line locations, sign positions. Genesys International's recent India launch

covers 1 lakh km of national corridors.

ISR

Intelligence, Surveillance, Reconnaissance. Defence-domain category for imagery and

signal-collection systems.

EW

Electronic Warfare. Defence-domain sub-category covering jamming, spoofing, RF detection.

CVTMS

Commercial Vehicle Tracking and Monitoring System. Telematics for fleet operators. IOCL's Rs.

110 cr CVTMS contract with MAPMYINDIA covers ~23,000 LPG trucks.

Regulators, Programs & Policy

IN-SPACe

Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre. The autonomous body created to

permit and promote private-sector space activities. Issued the Rs. 1,200 cr private national EO

contract to the Pixxel-led consortium in January 2026.

NGP-2022

National Geospatial Policy 2022. Liberalised private-sector participation in geospatial data

and restricted full-resolution Indian-territory map data to Indian-controlled entities. The

structural moat behind MAPMYINDIA / Genesys.

SoI

Survey of India. The official national mapping agency. Awarded MAPMYINDIA the National

Geo-Spatial Platform contract — the first national platform for geospatial-data cataloging +

role-based access.

PIB

Press Information Bureau. The Government of India's official communications channel for

policy releases. Used here as a primary-source citation for NGP / Budget claims.

PM Gati Shakti

National master-plan for multi-modal infrastructure connectivity, launched 2021. Pulls

GIS-grade integration data across 16 ministries.

BharatNet

National rural broadband project. Pulls fibre + GIS + tower mapping at scale.

Smart Cities Mission

Programme to develop 100 cities with city-data + IoT integration. Pulls GIS at municipal scale.

DGCA

Directorate General of Civil Aviation. The drone-licensing and operations regulator in India.

PPP

Public-Private Partnership. The contracting structure of the IN-SPACe national EO award.

MeitY

Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology. India's central tech-policy department;

owns the data-centre tax-holiday eligibility scheme.

Companies, Tickers & Markets

NSE / BSE

National Stock Exchange / Bombay Stock Exchange. India's two main equity exchanges.

"NSE/BSE" means the stock is listed on both.

LTP

Last Traded Price. The most recent quoted market price for a stock.

DMA

Daily Moving Average. "50-DMA" = 50-day moving average. A common technical reference.

OPM

Operating Profit Margin. Operating profit / revenue. Used as a quality-of-earnings indicator.

PE

Price / Earnings ratio. Market cap divided by net profit. Higher PE = market is paying more for

each rupee of profit.

EBITDA

Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation, and Amortisation. A cash-profit proxy.

RPT

Related-Party Transaction. Money flow between the listed company and entities controlled by

promoters / directors / their families. Subject to disclosure rules.

Promoter

Indian-securities term for the founding owner / controlling shareholder group. Disclosed on a

per-stock basis.

Q3 FY26

Third quarter of fiscal year 2025-26 (Oct-Dec 2025). India's fiscal year runs April to March.

9MFY26

First nine months of fiscal year 2025-26 (Apr-Dec 2025).

OEM

Original Equipment Manufacturer. In auto context = the carmaker (Maruti, Tata Motors,

Mahindra, Hyundai etc.) buying embedded maps from MAPMYINDIA.

CAGR

Compound Annual Growth Rate. Annualised growth rate over multiple years.

PSU / Maharatna

Public Sector Undertaking. Government-owned listed company. Maharatna = top tier

(financial size + autonomy). BEL is a Maharatna PSU.

DRHP

Draft Red Herring Prospectus. The first formal IPO filing with SEBI. "No DRHP visible" means the

company has not started a public listing process.

SEBI

Securities and Exchange Board of India. The market regulator.

SME-graduated

A company that listed first on the SME (small-and-medium-enterprise) board with lighter

disclosure rules, then graduated to the main NSE/BSE board. Governance scrutiny is harder

during the SME period.

Executive Summary

The Indian geospatial opportunity is structurally real and policy-anchored. The geospatial

economy is projected at Rs. 63,100 crore by 2025 with ~14% CAGR onwards, the National Geospatial

Policy 2022 has formally unlocked private participation, and IN-SPACe has just (January 2026)

awarded a Rs. 1,200 crore private national Earth-Observation constellation to a consortium of

pre-IPO companies. The thesis is not in question. What is in question is whether the listed surface

of this theme is investable at scale today. The honest answer is: thinly.

CORE FINDING

Most of the cleanest mechanism plays are pre-IPO.

Most of the “famous-name” exposures (TCS, INFY, L&T, Delhivery) are dilution-grade and flunk the

mechanism-not-analogy test. The two listed names that survive a fundamental light-filter are

MAPMYINDIA and GENESYS at the data layer, with IDEAFORGE as the single listed UAV pure-play.

Everything else either dilutes the thesis, fails the peak-price gate, or is more honestly held for the

defense thesis.

Bottom-line picks per layer

• Mapping data: GENESYS INTERNATIONAL is the obvious peer-replication play (faster grower than MMI,

smaller base) — but Q3 FY26 fired exceptional charges and a revenue decline; multi-quarter watch.

• Earth Observation: No clean listed pure-play exists today. IPO-WATCH high priority for Pixxel, Dhruva

Space, SatSure. Listed BEL / Astra / MTAR are diluted.

• Drones & aerial imagery: IDEAFORGE is the only listed pure-play with a sector tailwind — chart healed

favourably for mean-reversion, but profitability still emerging; watchlist not buy-now.

• Infrastructure cross-stack: HFCL and STLTECH (already in book) capture the fiber-backbone overlap

with the data-centre thesis — additive, not a fresh entry.

• Logistics, insurance, climate, surveying, IT services: honest empty-or-diluted-set. Skip as a thesis

driver.

Part I / The Strategic Context

1.1 Market sizing and the NGP-2022 unlock

The Indian geospatial economy is among the most policy-leveraged thematic exposures available in the

Indian listed market today. Three forces are operating in the same direction simultaneously:

data-sovereignty regulation that restricts foreign entities from holding full-resolution Indian map data,

private-participation liberalisation via the National Geospatial Policy 2022, and structural demand from PM

Gati Shakti, BharatNet, Smart Cities, defense modernisation, and the auto-OEM ADAS transition. Each force

on its own would be material; the combination creates a rare regulatory wedge favouring domestic

incumbents.

Market sizing reads as follows: the geospatial economy is projected at Rs. 63,100 crore by 2025 [Secondary,

Mordor / Allied / Business Standard], with subsequent CAGR estimates clustering around 13–15% through

  1. Growth is uneven across the value chain — foundational mapping data is mature and concentrated,

downstream analytics and EO-as-a-service are nascent and fragmented, and the satellite layer was

effectively closed to private capital until NGP-2022.

ANCHOR FACT · PRIMARY

January 2026: IN-SPACe awarded the national private EO contract.

Pixxel-led consortium — with Dhruva Space, PierSight, and SatSure as partners — won the

IN-SPACe national EO PPP. Rs. 1,200 crore allocated; 12 satellites over 5 years; hyperspectral +

optical + SAR + multispectral. Losing bids: Astra Microwave + BEL, and GalaxEye + CoreEL (~Rs. 97

crore). This is the single most important data point in the theme — it confirms that the EO private

layer is now real, contracted, and concentrated in four pre-IPO names.

1.2 Why the listed surface is thin

There are two reasons the listed surface looks thin relative to the size of the theme. First, the most

concentrated capability is still pre-IPO — Pixxel, Dhruva Space, SatSure, PierSight, GalaxEye, Cropin, Garuda

Aerospace, and IG Drones have no public DRHP visible as of this write-up. Second, several “obvious” listed

exposures are mechanism-free: TCS, Infosys, HCL, Wipro, LTIMindtree, and Persistent all maintain

“geospatial / location-intelligence” service practices, but combined revenue from those practices is

plausibly under 2% of total revenue at any of them [Secondary, no public segment break-out]. Owning a

$200B IT-services company for a $50M GIS practice is the canonical analogy-not-mechanism failure

mode that PROCESSES.md §5.22 was written to catch.

The same applies to logistics (Delhivery, Blue Dart) and insurance (ICICI Lombard and general insurers).

These are GIS consumers — mapping-cost reductions show up as opex savings or product features, not as

a revenue line on the income statement. There is no GIS-specific P&L at any of them. Treating them as GIS

exposure is the same failure mode in a different costume.
