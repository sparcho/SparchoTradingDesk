§3 What the sector is telling you

  1. Volume growth exists, margins don't. Delhivery grew revenue 28% and profit fell 65%; Allcargo Express grew 13.5% at a 6.2% EBITDA margin. Diesel and wage costs are eating volume growth. A fall in crude (peace headline) is the single biggest positive for the express and road names.
  2. CONCOR's flat top line for four quarters says EXIM container volumes are not growing — the Hormuz closure and global slowdown show up here first.
  3. Energy logistics is the outlier and it is war-geared. Aegis's LPG distribution margin went from ~₹4,000/tonne historically to ₹7,000+ (one analyst's Q2 base case is ₹12,000). That is a scarcity premium. Management says ₹7,000+ survives normalisation; the market will test that on the first credible Hormuz reopening.
  4. The cleanest balance-sheet signal in the sector is VRL's buyback at ₹320 (record date 22 Oct) — a company paying 11.6% above market for its own stock.

§4 Positioning read for the desk

  • Long the "peace" leg: VRLLOG, MAHLOG, DELHIVERY, BLUEDART gain most from lower diesel. Only VRL has the tape to act on now.
  • Short-the-peace risk: AEGISLOG, AEGISVOPAK, CONFIPET. Hold or buy only at structure, sized for a margin-normalisation gap.
  • eClerx is not in this trade. It's a rupee-depreciation and USD-billing name with results due late Oct — see its own brief.
