§1 Executive summary

Denta is a Bengaluru water-EPC contractor whose entire order book sits in Karnataka and whose paymaster is,

for ~84% of revenue, the Government of Karnataka. On the surface it is the cheapest profitable listed water-EPC

name in India — ~13x trailing earnings, 1.5x book, almost no debt. Underneath, the thing the market is pricing

is not the P/E. It is that ₹114 Cr of profit over FY25–FY26 produced −₹106 Cr of operating cash flow:

the ₹150 Cr of IPO working-capital money has been absorbed into inventory, unbilled work and receivables, and

working-capital days have gone from 87 (FY24) to 380 (FY26) against a guided 95–120.

That would be a timing problem in a healthy state budget. It is a structural one in this budget:

Karnataka's own finance department flagged ₹36,136 Cr of pending contractor bills on 22 Sep 2026 and called

borrowing headroom "virtually exhausted"; the contractors' association puts dues at ~₹38,000 Cr, of which

~₹14,000 Cr is Water Resources and ~₹3,600 Cr Minor Irrigation — Denta's two core client departments.

Central JJM money barely moved in FY26 (RE cut 75% vs BE) and FY27 releases are under 10% of budget so far.

Profitability is also normalising, not just cash: EBITDA margin has gone 38% → 33% → 34% → 30% (FY23–FY26) and

22.4% in Q1 FY27, with PAT −40% YoY. Management has missed its own guidance three times in a row

(FY26 ₹300 Cr target dropped, Q4 revenue ₹55 Cr vs ~₹65 Cr guided, working-capital days 380 vs 95–120), and FY27's

"20%+ growth" now needs Q2–Q4 revenue up ~32% YoY after a −13% Q1.

Verdict line. Not a stock to build in, and not one where a quick round-trip back above the high-270s should be

assumed. The near-term tape is oversold and supply has dried up, so a relief bounce into 268–273 is the

higher-probability move within two weeks; a clean run back through 278–286 needs a catalyst the calendar

does not currently show. The Q2 print (mid-Nov) is a negative-skew event: seasonally the weakest execution

quarter, against a margin and cash trend that has not turned.

§4 The core issue — cash conversion

  1. Profit is not cash. FY25–26 cumulative PAT ~₹114 Cr vs CFO ~−₹106 Cr. The IPO's ₹150 Cr working-capital

allocation was spent exactly as stated — and is now sitting in unbilled work and inventory.

  1. The monitoring agency flagged ₹169.5 Cr outstanding on four projects with completed work held in inventory

because billing/collection was slow (Feb 2026 call).

  1. Rating guard-rails: CARE BBB (Stable) / A3+, reaffirmed 06 Apr 2026. Negative triggers: receivables >90% of

revenue, or liquidity <₹50 Cr. Receivables incl. unbilled were 31% (FY24) → 57% (FY25) → 65% (Dec 2025);

liquidity ~₹86.5 Cr (Dec 2025). Neither trigger is hit, but both are moving the wrong way.

  1. What would flip it: a quarter with positive CFO and inventory/unbilled falling. The Q2 (H1) balance sheet in

mid-Nov is where this shows — it matters more than the P&L line.

§5 Governance — the discount the market will not drop

  • Chairman & ED C Mruthyunjaya Swamy is a retired senior Karnataka PWD official. A Lokayukta disproportionate-

assets case (2011 FIR) was quashed, reinstated by the Supreme Court (2018), and prosecution sanction refused (2019);

ED closed its case in 2022 (DRHP addendum). The IPO papers said he had "never been part of management"; he became

Chairman & ED four months after listing. His wife is an ED; his son-in-law is Whole-time Director and CFO.

  • Pre-IPO anonymous complaints to SEBI/MCA (alleging benami directors, inflated costs) — denied; complainants untraceable.
  • Related parties: R P Shetty Engineers, JNS Infra Projects / JNS Constructions, the Chairman's HUF consultancy,

Uva Sands, rent to promoters. The MD is described as the former head of Shri JN Shetty Class-I Contractors; the

July JNS sub-contract (₹44.67 Cr) is a related-party order (filing says arm's length).

  • Two CFO resignations pre-IPO. An independent director is a former Minor Irrigation executive engineer — i.e.

the client department.

None of this is proof of wrongdoing. All of it is why a 13x multiple has not attracted a single institution back.

§6 Sector backdrop — the payer is the problem

Jal Jeevan Mission. Extended (JJM 2.0, Mar 2026, to Dec 2028), total outlay ₹8.69 lakh Cr. But FY26 RE was

₹17,000 Cr vs ~₹67,000 Cr BE (−75%), PRS notes no central JJM funds released to states in FY26, and FY27

releases by early Aug were ~₹6,150 Cr (<10% of BE). Contractors sector-wide are owed ₹30–35k Cr (Electrosteel est.).

Listed casualties: Ion Exchange stopped taking JJM work (Q4 margin 2.3%); VPRPL loss-making, −85% in a year,

87.7% promoter pledge; Afcons Q1 profit ₹30 Cr from ₹137 Cr.

Karnataka. Capex outlay FY27 BE ₹71,924 Cr, but FY26 was cut 8% at RE and the water-supply line was cut 43% at RE.

Guarantee schemes ₹51,286 Cr (12% of spend); committed spending 59% of revenue receipts. On 22 Sep 2026 the finance

department flagged ₹36,136 Cr of pending bills across PWD/WRD/MI/RDPR, liabilities at 24.94% of GSDP vs a 25% cap, and

advised holding new projects. The contractors' association (3 Oct) claims ~₹38,000 Cr due — **WRD ~₹14,000 Cr,

MI ~₹3,600 Cr** — has a November deadline and has threatened district protests. The state released ₹2,400 Cr to

irrigation in early October by pulling forward next-quarter allocation — a small, real positive for collections.

Read-across. The market has separated water EPC by payer: Wabag (multilateral/central, ₹950 Cr net cash)

+39% in a year; the state-JJM cohort (VPRPL, Enviro Infra, EMS, Denta) de-rated hard. Denta is −23% YTD, −42% 1Y,

well below the small-cap index — this is a sector/payer de-rating, not just market beta.

§12 What would change the view

Better: positive H1 CFO with inventory/unbilled falling; GoK clearing WRD/MI arrears; the ~₹400 Cr order; a

non-Karnataka win (Madhya Pradesh / Odisha were named); promoter holding unchanged or rising.

Worse: promoter selling in the Sep pattern; CARE outlook change; receivables approaching 90% of revenue; liquidity

<₹50 Cr; another related-party order; a daily close below 239.

§13 Risks (Denta-specific)

  1. Single state, single payer — revenue follows GoK's cash, not Denta's execution.
  2. Working capital at 380 days; IPO cash largely consumed; CFO negative two years running.
  3. Governance and related-party concentration (Chairman history, family CFO, JN Shetty-linked entities).
  4. Repeated guidance misses; margins normalising from an unusually high base.
  5. Execution delays (10 of 17 projects delayed at IPO) → liquidated damages / bank-guarantee risk.
  6. Thin, retail-only float — moves are sharp both ways; no institutional bid underneath.
