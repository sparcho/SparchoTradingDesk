Plain-English Glossary

Company-research briefs accumulate jargon. This page expands every abbreviation and technical term

used in this document so the reader does not need to know what CPaaS or DLT is to follow the argument.

Terms grouped by category. Everything else in the brief is written in plain English on top of these definitions.

Communications-Platform-as-a-Service (CPaaS)

CPaaS

Communications Platform as a Service. Cloud APIs that let businesses send SMS, voice, RCS,

WhatsApp, email at scale to their customers. TANLA is India's largest CPaaS by volume.

A2P SMS

Application-to-Person SMS. Bank OTPs, e-com order confirmations, food-delivery alerts. The

legacy revenue base for any CPaaS company.

RCS

Rich Communication Services. Google's modern SMS replacement — branded sender, rich

media, interactive cards. Slowly displacing A2P SMS in some categories.

OTT messaging

Over-The-Top messaging that bypasses telecom SMS rails — WhatsApp Business, iMessage

Business, Google RCS Business. The structural threat to CPaaS economics.

ARPU

Average Revenue Per User. Used by both telcos and platform companies; in TANLA's Indosat

Wisely AI case, +10% client ARPU reflects monetization uplift.

NRR

Net Revenue Retention. Recurring revenue from existing customers versus one year ago,

including expansions and minus churn. Above 100% = customers expanding.

Gross margin

Revenue minus direct cost of carrier-terminating fees and bandwidth. CPaaS gross margins

are typically 25-40% because terminating-carrier costs eat the rest.

Wisely AI & the AI Layer

Wisely AI

TANLA's flagship AI-driven anti-spam / anti-scam / anti-fraud platform. Marketed as a

cyber-defense layer for telecom carriers. Indosat (Indonesia) deployment Q4 FY26 protected

100M users from 2B spam events.

DLT

Distributed Ledger Technology. India's TRAI-mandated framework for telco-side

bulk-message regulation. Makes Indian telco operators verify principal-entity / template /

consent before delivery. Creates incumbents-favoring moat for licensed messaging-platform

players.

LLM-native

messaging

Hypothetical near-future where Anthropic / OpenAI / Google bundle

messaging-orchestration into their enterprise AI offerings, taking the platform layer away

from incumbents like TANLA. Real long-term threat.

Vertical AI

AI capability targeted at one industry (telecom, banking, e-commerce) versus a horizontal

model. TANLA's pivot is from horizontal CPaaS to vertical comms-AI for telecom operators.

Section 3 / The Wisely AI Pivot — Real Moat or

Marketing?

This is the load-bearing question for any forward-looking TANLA thesis. The core CPaaS business (A2P SMS)

is structurally pressured by OTT messaging displacement; the question is whether Wisely AI is a genuine

new platform business (which would re-rate the multiple) or a marketing wrapper around incremental

product features (which would not).

3.1 What Wisely AI does, technically

Wisely AI is a real-time message-classification engine deployed at the telco-carrier level (not at the

brand-sender or end-user level). It intercepts inbound A2P traffic at the SMSC / OTT gateway, classifies

each message through an AI model trained on labeled telecom-spam / scam / fraud patterns, and either

delivers, flags, or blocks. The telco pays a per-million-message fee plus a base-platform license. The

pricing is subscription-with-usage, NOT one-time license.

Architecture: TANLA's training corpus draws from its own ~200B annual message volume (largest in India),

giving it a data-network-effect that smaller competitors cannot replicate. Inference runs on TANLA's own

infrastructure (no Anthropic / OpenAI API dependency for the core classifier — material risk-management

decision per Q4 conf-call notes).

Section 4 / Core CPaaS Business Risk — The OTT Threat

TANLA's fortunes for the next 3-5 years depend more on the speed of CPaaS category disruption than on

Wisely AI scaling. This section quantifies the pressure surface and the company's defensibility.

4.1 The displacement thesis — and the data

Globally, A2P SMS volumes have been flat-to-declining since 2023 as WhatsApp Business, Google RCS

Business Messaging, and iMessage Business siphon transactional notifications away from carrier SMS rails.

India has lagged this trend because of two structural factors: (1) DLT regulation requires carrier-side

verification of every A2P message, which OTT players don't yet provide at scale; (2) RCS Business adoption

has been slow in India relative to NAM / EU markets.

TANLA's India SMS volumes have grown ~10-12% YoY through FY25-FY26 even as global SMS has declined.

The DLT moat is real and quantifiable. But: WhatsApp Business is now ~12-15% of India's

transactional-notification volume and growing 35% YoY. The crossover point — where OTT message

volume exceeds traditional SMS for transactional use cases — is plausibly 2028-2030 in India per industry

estimates.

4.2 The killer test — if WhatsApp Business goes free

Meta has not signaled this, but the structural risk: if WhatsApp Business messaging becomes free for

transactional alerts (zero per-message cost vs. TANLA's ~₹0.10-0.20 per SMS), the economic incentive to

use carrier SMS for OTPs / order-confirmations / delivery alerts collapses. India's OTT messaging

penetration (90%+) means substitution would be fast.

Mitigation: A2P SMS has reach-coverage advantages for unbanked / non-smart-phone users (~25-30% of

India's adult population), and DLT compliance gives the regulator a legitimate basis to throttle OTT

substitution. But the secular direction is one-way.

TANLA at ₹485 (market cap ~₹6,500 Cr) requires the following assumptions to justify the valuation under a

~₹3,500 Cr at ~13% EBITDA margin. Fair value at 7x EV/EBITDA: ₹290-330 — a 32-40% downside from current.
