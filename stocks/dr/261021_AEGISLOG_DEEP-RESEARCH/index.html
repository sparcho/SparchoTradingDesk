Capex and project execution (this is the "order book" for a terminal company):

  • FY27 capex of about $1.2 bn. The pipeline to FY31 is about $5 bn, at target gearing of about 0.6x. Borrowings fell from ₹4,606 Cr to ₹4,150 Cr.
  • JNPA (₹1,675 Cr): phase 1, about 100,000 m³, is due Q3 FY27. A 52,000 t refrigerated LPG tank has been approved.
  • Pipavav ammonia (36,000 t): commissioned, with a 15-year take-or-pay contract with Hindustan Zinc. ITOCHU holds 10% and is going to 25%.
  • Other projects: Mumbai 64,000 m³, Kochi, Mangalore LPG and Kandla. Vadhavan is a non-binding MoU of about ₹20,000 Cr.
  • Distribution guidance is 0.5 → 0.75 mt, then >1 mt this year, 1.5 mt next year and 2 mt eventually.

Guidance credibility. Volumes are running ahead of plan. The margin is the open question:

  • Historically it was about ₹4,000/t. Management guides ₹7,000+/t and says that level can be sustained after normalisation.
  • One analyst's Q2 base case is ₹12,000/t, with a range of ₹10,000–15,000/t.
  • Every ₹1,000/t on about 290k t is roughly ₹29 Cr of quarterly EBITDA.

§2 The war problem

Aegis gains from disruption: it is LPG scarcity that pays the margin. A credible Hormuz reopening is a negative headline for the stock even if volumes keep growing, because the market will price the margin back toward ₹4,000–7,000/t. This makes Aegis the opposite of the desk's peace-leg names.
