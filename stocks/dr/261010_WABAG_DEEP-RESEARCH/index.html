§1 Executive summary

"Water should do well" is true — but in India in 2026 the market is paying for who signs the cheque, not for water.

Wabag and Denta sit on the same theme and opposite sides of that line:

  • Wabag is paid by multilateral lenders (JICA, ADB, EIB, World Bank), Gulf sovereign utilities (Saudi SWA/NWC,

Kuwait MEWRE), central schemes and private industry (Reliance, BPCL, GAIL). It has publicly stopped bidding for

state-paid JJM work. Result: record ₹19,394 Cr order book (~4.7x revenue), revenue +20%, **net cash ₹965 Cr for

14 straight quarters, AA-/Stable rating, every covering broker at Buy. Stock +48% YTD** in a market that is down ~14%.

  • Denta is paid by one state government whose finance department says it is out of borrowing room. Result: two

years of negative operating cash flow, 380 working-capital days, Q1 profit −40%, stock −23% YTD.

But Wabag is not a clean "buy the quality" call at ₹1,937. Three things the headline hides:

  1. Q1's margin was flattered by the rupee. Reported EBITDA margin 13.1% includes a ₹36.8 Cr forex gain. Strip it

out and core EBITDA was ~₹80 Cr, ~9% margin vs ~15% a year earlier. Forex + other income + associates were ~49% of

Q1 pre-tax profit. A weak rupee (≈95–97/$) helps; a rebound would reverse it.

  1. Cash conversion has slipped: FY26 operating cash flow ₹207 Cr = 0.56x PAT (FY25: ₹355 Cr, 1.2x). Debtor days

~234; non-current receivables rising (₹595 → ₹725 Cr in six months to Sep-25).

  1. ~35–40% of the order book is Middle East, during an active US–Iran conflict with Hormuz disrupted. Management

says no material impact and has issued force-majeure letters; the stock has fallen 16.5% from its 11 Sep high as

that escalated.

Verdict line. Wabag is the best-quality listed water name in India and the one most likely to still be compounding in

  1. At ~30x trailing / ~25x forward it is priced for that, and it is in the middle of a pullback with momentum not yet

turned. The better entry is lower (₹1,850–1,900 first, ₹1,680–1,775 if the market breaks), not a chase.

As a destination for money coming out of a state-paid water EPC, it is the right kind of water exposure; the question

is only price and timing.

What the gap in multiples is paying for. Denta's 13x is cheap only if its receivables turn into cash at face value.

Wabag's 30x is expensive only if its ex-forex margin stays at 9%. The first is a question about a state budget;

the second is a question about one quarter's mix (EP→EPC shift, expected-credit-loss provisions that reverse on

collection). The second is the easier problem to have.

§10 Risks

  1. Earnings quality: ~49% of Q1 PBT from forex, other income and associates; ex-forex EBITDA fell ~27% YoY.
  2. Gulf concentration (~35–40% of book) during an active conflict — force majeure, shipping, insurance, payment delays.
  3. Currency: reported margins depend on a weak rupee.
  4. Working capital: debtor days ~234, rising non-current receivables, OCF down 42% in FY26.
  5. Fixed-price EPC desal (Perur, Yanbu, Kuwait) — execution and cost-overrun risk; EP→EPC shift lowers gross margin.
  6. Valuation: ~30x trailing after +48% YTD; every broker already at Buy.
  7. Competition in Gulf desal (Acciona, Veolia/Suez, Chinese and Korean EPCs); Ion Exchange's margin collapse hints at

industrial-water price pressure.

  1. Low promoter holding (19%); ₹87 Cr customs demand contested.
