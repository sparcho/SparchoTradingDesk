The Orbit

The ring, developed. Every dimension now carries meaning — including the angle, which in v1 carried none. It shows the active book at a glance, the closed record on the rim, the names still waiting on a fib read outside it, and the headline numbers at the centre. Hover anything.

Read it in one sentence How far out a node sits is how much room it has above your stop. Where it sits around the ring is when you entered it — newest at 12 o'clock, going back in time anticlockwise. The tail is where it was five sessions ago, so you can see which way it is travelling. Everything else is supporting detail.

Rim ticksthe last 24 closed trades, newest at 12. Outward = won, inward = lost.
Centrebanked result and win rate. The green arc is the win rate.
What the shape tells you before you read anything A tight cluster near the middle means the book is stretched — several positions with little room above their stops. A wide scatter means it is comfortable. Tails pointing inward across several nodes is the early warning that matters: the book is deteriorating together, which is what a correlated drawdown looks like before it shows up in the P&L. None of that requires reading a number.
Two things I would still watch First, with more than about a dozen open positions the ring will crowd near 12 o'clock if several were entered the same week — I have spread same-day entries slightly so they do not overlap, but past ~15 live names this needs a second band rather than more squeezing. Second, radius is measured in R, not in percent, so a node's distance is comparable across names of any price — that is deliberate, and it is also the thing most likely to confuse at first glance, which is why the legend leads with it.
