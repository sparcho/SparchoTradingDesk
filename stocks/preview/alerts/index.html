04how it reads
four states, and the sentence each one writes
statewhenwhat it means, in words
heavy · absorbing 5× volume or more, and the feed itself calls the travel absorbing (under its measured 0.81× line) A lot of stock is changing hands and the price is not going with it. Someone is selling into the buying.
heavy · moving 5× volume or more, and the feed calls the travel displacing (over its measured 1.24× line) The volume is genuinely repricing it. A move with something behind it.
heavy · under its average 5× volume or more, travel is mixed, but price is below its own average price for the day No clean travel verdict, but it is trading under its VWAP on heavy volume — the shape of selling into strength. This is what 04-Sep looked like.
heavy · mixed 5× volume or more, travel mixed, price above its average Big volume, no clean verdict either way. Look, do not conclude.
busy above the feed’s own alive line (1.5×) but under 5× More active than usual. Worth a glance, not an alarm.
not alive the feed did not flag it today Nothing unusual. Collapsed to one muted line so the band stays short on ordinary days.
The absorbing / moving verdict is the feed’s, not mine. Those bands were measured on 3,969 name-sessions and this page quotes them rather than re-deriving them. The only judgement added here is the 5× “heavy” cut, and that one is unmeasured — see below.
Every card also carries the pressure flip when there is one: the split between how the whole session has traded and how the last half hour has traded. A name that is 55% buyers all day but 38% buyers in the last thirty minutes is turning, and that is a different message from the headline volume number.
Two honest limits, stated up front.
1. The 5× “heavy” line is mine, and it is unmeasured — I picked it to separate the 04-Sep shape from an ordinary busy day. The feed says the same thing about its own 1.5× alive line in its own payload: “alive_rvol and liquidity_floor are UNVALIDATED defaults — move them from the journal, never from opinion.” Treat both as placeholders until they are measured against outcomes.
2. The feed publishes only the names it flagged as alive. So for a held name that is absent, “quiet today” and “never measured” look identical from here. The band says not alive rather than quiet for that reason. Closing this properly means the producer publishing the list of names it measured, not just the ones that fired.
05why it is built this way
The unlock gate is the privacy boundary, and that is not a preference. The public volume feed ships book_disclosure: “none” on purpose — which name you own is never published. A cloud-side “alert me when one of my names goes alive” would have to filter public code on a private list, and filtering on a private identifier publishes it. So the whole cross is done in your browser, after decryption: the held list only exists there. Same pattern as the trade ledger.

No push notifications. The desk stays the one place things are said. Nothing is emailed, nothing buzzes.

No second password, no flicker. The band reads the payload the desk has already decrypted for the Portfolio and Radar tabs — it never asks again, and it renders in place on the normal 60-second refresh rather than being inserted after the fact, so the page does not jump under you.
06what it does not do
It does not tell you to buy or sell. It reports what the tape is doing on names you own, in words, and the decision stays yours. It cannot see intraday order flow beyond half-hour buckets, it has no depth, and on a thinly traded name a large relative number can be one order. It is a look at this signal, not a trade signal — which is the same conclusion the volume study reached: volume finds range, not direction.
