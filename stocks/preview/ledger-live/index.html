The live ledger — hover a node, the trade opens

Both views answer the same brief: the trade list becomes a live graph, and detail appears on hover rather than as a wall of rows. Both carry the performance figure, the open trades, the closed record and the names still blocked on a fib read. Pick one — or tell me which half of each to keep.

What the references were saying All three share one grammar: a centre that holds the single most important number, and curved connectors carrying state outward to labelled nodes. The cyber-defence board is the closest to our problem — one score, lines fanning to entities, colour telling you which need you. That is exactly a book of open trades. So neither mockup invents a new language; they apply that one to the ledger.

Why the spine reads fastest The eye gets a single ordered column — one scan, top to bottom, no grid to parse. The pill carries the number, the line carries the state, and position in the column is the ranking. The amber line is visible from the centre outward, so “something needs me” is answered before you read a single word. Detail only appears when you ask for it, which is what stops the page being a wall.
B

The Ring

distance from centre = distance from your stop

The centre is your stop. A node’s distance from it is how much room the trade has — so anything near the middle is in trouble, and the outer band is winning. Angle just spaces them out. This encodes risk spatially: you read the book’s exposure without reading any number.

winning at or through the stop needs a decision waiting — not entered node size = position size
What the ring buys, and what it costs It is the only layout here where the shape of the picture is itself the risk report: a cluster hugging the middle means the book is stretched, a wide scatter means it is comfortable, and you see that before reading. The cost is that angle carries no meaning, which is a real weakness — a viewer will look for one. The spine has no such dead dimension. My recommendation is A for daily use, with the ring as a once-a-day “how exposed am I” glance if you want it.
