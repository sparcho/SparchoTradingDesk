Master ledger — the revamp

Every number on this page is invented. It exists to show the shape, not your positions — a public preview URL must never carry the book. Section order is the one you asked for: performance → open positions → ledger. “Needs attention” is gone as a section; it lives on the cards now.

The problem being fixed The ledger is a 13-column table. A table is the right shape for data you look up and the wrong shape for data you read — and you never look up a trade by ID, you scan for what happened. Three specific things made it unreadable: cryptic glyphs (E ▸ T1 ▸ T2 · S), six machine status words for four human states, and colour used decoratively so it carried no information.
Decision 1 — the words The desk emitted OPEN, FILLED, ACTIVE, PLANNED, CLOSED, REVIEWED, plus watch and missed. That is eight tokens for four real situations, and the difference between ACTIVE and REVIEWED is an internal bookkeeping detail you should never have to hold in your head.
OPEN · FILLED · ACTIVE→Live — money is in it right now
PLANNED→Waiting — a level is set, price hasn’t come
CLOSED · REVIEWED→Closed — it finished, and the result is the point
no-fill · expired→Never filled — the plan expired untouched, which is not a loss
Decision 2 — kill the glyph, draw the trade E ▸ T1 ▸ T2 · S ◤ asks you to decode five symbols to learn one thing: where price is between the stop and the target. That is a picture, so it should be drawn. The bar below is the single most repeated element in the design — stop on the left, target on the right, your entry marked, and the bright handle is where price is now. One glance answers “am I winning, and how much room is left”.
Decision 3 — colour carries state, nothing else Green means it made money. Red means it lost. Amber means a decision is waiting on you and appears nowhere else on the page, which is what lets “Needs attention” stop being a section — an amber card in the open-positions grid is the attention, in the place you were already looking. Cyan means live. Grey means nothing is happening. No other colour appears.

Decision 4 — “Needs attention” is not a section any more It was a list that duplicated the ledger and demanded a look even when the honest answer was “hold”. A position either needs a decision or it doesn’t, and that belongs on the position. So the state moved onto the card: amber border, amber badge, and the two buttons that resolve it. Four calm cards and one amber one is a faster read than a rail that says “5 now” and makes you open each to find out none of them mattered. The header count still tells you at a glance: 1 needs a decision · 4 holding.

Decision 5 — the expanded view is a paragraph, then the evidence You said the detail is “too complicated to look at and understand even for me”, and the reason is that it opens with five numbered bands of instrumentation. Nobody reads instrumentation first — they want to know what happened. So the story goes first, in one paragraph, in the same words you’d use out loud. The six numbers you actually reach for sit under it. Everything else — the ladder drawing, the stop-policy counterfactuals, the level provenance — is real and worth keeping, so it moves behind one line you can open. Nothing was deleted; it was ordered.
Decision 6 — “never filled” stops polluting the record A plan that expired untouched used to sit in the same visual channel as a loss. It isn’t one: no money was ever at risk. It now has its own hollow marker and an em-dash instead of a result, so scanning the dot column gives you an honest read of the class — won, lost, live, or never happened.
What I did NOT change, and why The content is right — you said so, and the study agrees. Entry, stop, targets, R, MAE/MFE, hold time, stop-policy counterfactuals and level provenance all stay in the payload and all stay reachable. This is a presentation change only: nothing is removed from the data, and no number here is computed differently. The one behavioural change is the section order and the disappearance of the attention rail, both of which you asked for.
